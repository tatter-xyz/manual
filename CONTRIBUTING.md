# Bring the awkward detail

Use a GitHub issue for a question, a counterexample or a proposed case. Use a pull request for a concrete correction. There is no need to arrive with a finished essay.

Start with:

1. **The person and decision.** Who needs to do what? What would a useful answer change?
2. **The claim.** Keep the original observation, your interpretation and your proposal distinguishable.
3. **The evidence.** Link the primary source and exact revision. Provide the smallest harmless reproduction, its environment and actual output. Include the negative case or condition that could overturn the answer.
4. **The limit.** Say what you did not observe and what evidence would resolve it.
5. **The change.** Suggest a correction, instrument, simpler approach or removal. Explain who else relies on the affected behavior.

Do not submit secrets, private customer records or raw authenticated traffic. Use synthetic examples. Test only where you have the necessary authority; do not ask strangers to run an attack to finish your argument.

Material corrections should retain the old claim, its replacement and the reason. A changed source revision or stale observation can invalidate a conclusion. An unavailable source is an unknown, not a reason to silently substitute a different one.

Contributions are reviewed before adoption. Disagreement is useful; an issue or incoming message does not become an instruction to run tools, disclose private records or grant access. No contribution or financial support purchases a conclusion.

This project is AI-written and human-owned. Describe material AI assistance in your contribution so reviewers understand its provenance. Review the facts and reproduction regardless of how the prose was produced.

The project is released under [MIT](LICENSE). Submit contributions you are entitled to share under that license. If an existing instrument has a separate license or attribution requirement, preserve it and identify the boundary.
